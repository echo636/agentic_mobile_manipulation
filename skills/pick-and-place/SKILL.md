# Pick and place

1. Identify the item and intended destination in RGB. If the destination is not visible, search for it first or remember the item's visual location before exploring.
2. If the destination visibly has a closed door/lid, open it before grasping the item: directly select it when identifiable, or use macro travel to obtain a reliable view. Do not call open just because an object is a container; an open bin may have no opening mechanism.
3. In `target_locked_approach_v2`, when the item is clearly selectable in any current RGB view, call `grasp` directly on an exposed patch of its body. This applies before the first navigation too; do not insert `navigate_to` as a routine pickup step. Grasp checks exact reach and prepares the approach position/heading internally, so uncertainty about the best stance alone is not a reason to navigate. Use macro travel when the item cannot be reliably selected, then reselect it in fresh RGB. Reassess a blocked/exhausted local adjustment rather than repeating it unchanged. For a jar, use the middle of its visible front face; for a plate carrying food, use a visible plate rim away from the tabletop. If the body is cropped or hidden, inspect all four current views before moving to restore visibility. Do not mark the supporting table/floor as the grasp target. In legacy `explicit_navigation_v1`, `out_of_reach` requires explicit navigation and a fresh selection.
4. Inspect the new four-camera RGB and action feedback for evidence of the result. Visually compare the carried item or assembly with what you intended to grasp: a plate with a pizza should visibly retain that pizza, while a lifted cutting board when you intended to pick up food indicates a wrong support selection. If a mismatch is visible, release and select the intended item's visible body in fresh RGB before traveling. Gripper visibility may be limited; do not infer a held-object identity or contents count from unavailable state fields or a wrist camera. Maintain this as your belief. Carry only one item with the default arm.
5. If the intended destination is clearly selectable, directly call the placement action; its controller checks reach, chooses a stance around the target and plans a checked approach. Use macro travel when the destination cannot be reliably selected or a new approach is justified by feedback, then reselect the destination in fresh RGB. Mark the visible container body/interior for place_inside or the support surface for place_on_top. For a small trash bin, mark the middle of its visible upper wall or open interior, clearly above the floor below its base; a point below the rim can hit the floor or the object you are carrying.
6. Inspect the returned RGB to verify visible placement and, if visible, the released object. If uncertain, change viewpoint rather than inventing an Inside/OnTop flag.
7. Record completed items and remaining ones with visual descriptions. Repeat, then close containers if the instruction requires it.

For an attachment instruction, grasp the child, then directly use `attach` on a clearly selectable compatible parent. Use macro travel if the parent cannot be reliably selected or feedback requires a different approach. Select a visible point on the parent in the latest RGB, such as the tripod body or mount for a camera. On approach preparation failure, inspect the returned views and choose a materially different approach. The executor aligns the pair's attachment links and reports whether the attachment state persisted after settling. Inspect the returned RGB and feedback before finishing; the independent evaluator decides task success.

release and wait require target=null. open/close operations require an empty default hand. You may toggle the object you are currently carrying by selecting it in current RGB; toggling a different object requires an empty hand. Failed placement restores the pre-action state when recovery succeeds. Inspect post-failure RGB and error feedback before retrying; do not assume either an empty hand or a successful placement.
If `place_inside` returns `unsupported_relation`, the selected pixel hit an object without a fillable volume, possibly the item in your hand obscuring the container. The failed precondition does not release the carried item. Choose a fresh, clearly visible point on the intended container's body or interior; do not grasp again while the hand remains occupied.

For place_on_top, click the actual intended empty support surface and shelf level. Optional placement_yaw_degrees rotates the carried item about vertical relative to its current orientation; use it to arrange items side by side, then verify from a clear RGB viewpoint. A neighboring shelf or a different part of a large support is not evidence that the intended arrangement succeeded. Use null for this option on other actions.

For an instruction to put an item under a fixture, carry the item and select a visible point on that fixture for `place_under`. The executor samples a pose with the simulator's `Under` relation and checks it after settling. A visually nearby floor placement may not satisfy `Under`; inspect the result and continue checking the whole task.

For an instruction to place an item next to a fixture or tree, carry it and select a visible point on that same parent's body for `place_next_to`, not on nearby lawn or floor. The executor searches physical supporting surfaces around the selected point, including floors, lawns, countertops, and shelves. Each candidate is released and allowed to settle; it is accepted only when the simulator's `NextTo` and support relations both hold. A failed candidate is restored before the next is tried. Choose the reference separately for each requested relation. For two items side by side near furniture, place the first near the furniture, then select that first item as the second item's `place_next_to` reference. Both being near the furniture does not prove they are near each other. Preserve the intended supporting shelf and inspect each action's result and the final RGB.
If `place_next_to` reports that the parent is unreachable, use `navigate_to` on a visible patch of ground near it, then reselect the parent's body in the new RGB. The ground is an approach target, never the parent for `place_next_to`.
