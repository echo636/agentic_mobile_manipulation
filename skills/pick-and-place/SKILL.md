# Pick and place

1. Identify the item and intended destination in RGB. If the destination is not visible, search for it first or remember the item's visual location before exploring.
2. If the destination visibly has a closed door/lid, approach and open it before grasping the item. Do not call open just because an object is a container; an open bin may have no opening mechanism.
3. Approach the item by marking it in the current RGB. Reinspect after navigation; once its body is clearly visible, call `grasp` on a clearly exposed patch of that body rather than repeatedly navigating toward the same point. For a jar, use the middle of its visible front face. If food must stay on a plate, the plate is the object to grasp: select the white ceramic band outside the colored pizza/topping, on an exposed plate rim away from the tabletop. Check the actual marked pixel against that band before calling `grasp`; a point in the orange pizza center selects the pizza itself even if your intention was the plate. A thin plate at the edge of the image is easy to confuse with the table beneath it: change viewpoint to put the plate near the center and choose an unoccluded rim pixel before grasping. Grasping the food does not guarantee the required `OnTop(food, plate)` relation survives transport. Grasp has a bounded automatic approach to the selected point when needed; repeated public navigation may deliberately keep a small floor object at a visible standoff. If the body is cropped or hidden, change to a viewpoint that restores visibility. Do not mark the supporting table/floor.
   For an item recessed on a high cabinet shelf, first navigate to visible floor just outside the cabinet opening, then reselect the item's own visible face for `grasp`. A high shelf pixel is not a traversable navigation destination. If a grasp reports `navigation_unreachable`, a successful move to the floor in front of that shelf is recovery progress: inspect the new RGB and retry the grasp before concluding the item is unreachable.
   If an upper cabinet is over a counter, the floor directly below its door may be occupied by the counter and cannot be a base destination. Approach from the clear aisle along the counter or around its end, keeping the cabinet visible, then retry `open` or `grasp` on its own current RGB point. A failed floor navigation point near the counter does not prove that the cabinet is unreachable from another side.
   If the upper shelf is cropped after opening the cabinet, step back along the clear aisle and inspect a front or side camera that shows the full shelf before searching lower cabinets. A jar near the top image edge can disappear when you move too close. Avoid crossing the food preparation island merely to inspect the cabinet: nearby loose ingredients can be disturbed by robot motion.
4. Inspect the new four-camera RGB and action feedback for evidence of the result. Compare `contained_rigid_objects` with the intended carried assembly: a plate with one pizza should carry that pizza, while an unexpectedly large group means the clicked support may have been grasped. In that case, release and select the intended item's visible body in fresh RGB before traveling. Gripper visibility may be limited; do not infer held-object truth from an unavailable wrist camera. Maintain this as your belief. Carry only one item with the default arm.
   When collecting several loose items into a movable container, keep the container as the destination in your plan. Select an item outside it for each `grasp`, then select the container for `place_inside`. If RGB after a grasp indicates that you picked up the container, release it and reselect a loose item; moving the destination consumes actions and can hide or spill the remaining items.
   A toy box can leave the camera view when you turn toward an item on the same desk. Before concluding it moved, rotate or step back to reframe the desk. In the bedroom with athlete posters, the open toy box is the gray rectangular tub on the desktop; select its interior wall or rim, not the surrounding gray desk, wall, or floor. An `unsupported_relation` result on those surfaces means the box was not selected, so find the tub in fresh RGB before another `place_inside` attempt.
   For items inside an open basket, select a visible patch of the item above the basket rim. If the item is hidden by wicker or another item, change viewpoint before grasping; carrying the basket can scatter the items and obscure the remaining count.
   If a floor basket looks small and its contents are indistinguishable, use `navigate_to` on the basket body or rim itself, then inspect fresh front and side RGB. A floor point in front of it may leave the basket more than a metre away. If a `grasp` hits the fixed basket or floor, change to a different side and choose an exposed item pixel; do not count that attempt as an item removed.
5. Approach the destination using fresh RGB points. Once the destination is clearly visible and reachable, act on it instead of navigating closer. Mark the visible container body/interior for place_inside or the support surface for place_on_top. For a small trash bin, mark the middle of its visible upper wall or open interior, clearly above the floor below its base; a point below the rim can hit the floor or the object you are carrying.
   For a long bottom cabinet or drawer, select its near-side outer drawer face or opening lip for `place_inside`. A far interior/back-wall pixel can be on the same cabinet yet beyond the visible hand's reach. After an `out_of_reach` result, approach the near side and choose a new visible near-side cabinet point; repeating the deep pixel from another distant stance may fail again.
   For a refrigerator, select a visible point on the tall open refrigerator's interior or shelf, not a nearby sink, dishwasher, wall, or under-counter cabinet. If the refrigerator is cropped at the edge of the image, rotate or move until its full open body is in view before `place_inside`. An `unsupported_relation` on kitchen cabinetry means the selected pixel missed the refrigerator; reframe it in fresh RGB rather than trying another pixel on the same cabinet.
   If several items must share one container, keep a visual landmark for that specific destination and match it before every placement. Adjacent cabinets can look identical; verify the same door, shelf, and nearby objects in fresh RGB before selecting its pixel again.
6. Inspect the returned RGB to verify visible placement and, if visible, the released object. If uncertain, change viewpoint rather than inventing an Inside/OnTop flag.
7. Record completed items and remaining ones with visual descriptions. Repeat, then close containers if the instruction requires it.

For an attachment instruction, grasp the child, navigate to a visible approach to the compatible parent if needed, then use `attach` within reach. Select a visible point on the parent in the latest RGB, such as the tripod body or mount for a camera. If the executor reports `out_of_reach`, change viewpoint or select a different visible navigation approach before retrying. The executor aligns the pair's attachment links and reports whether the attachment state persisted after settling. Inspect the returned RGB and feedback before finishing; the independent evaluator decides task success.

release and wait require target=null. open/close operations require an empty default hand. You may toggle the object you are currently carrying by selecting it in current RGB; toggling a different object requires an empty hand. Failed placement restores the pre-action state when recovery succeeds. Inspect post-failure RGB and error feedback before retrying; do not assume either an empty hand or a successful placement.
If `place_inside` returns `unsupported_relation`, the selected pixel hit an object without a fillable volume, possibly the item in your hand obscuring the container. The failed precondition does not release the carried item. Choose a fresh, clearly visible point on the intended container's body or interior; do not grasp again while the hand remains occupied.

For place_on_top, click the actual intended empty support surface and shelf level. Optional placement_yaw_degrees rotates the carried item about vertical relative to its current orientation; use it to arrange items side by side, then verify from a clear RGB viewpoint. A neighboring shelf or a different part of a large support is not evidence that the intended arrangement succeeded. Use null for this option on other actions.
For a small candle on a dining table, navigate until a clear horizontal tabletop patch is visible beside the existing objects, then select that patch. From a distant low view, a window or table edge can overlap the apparent tabletop; a failed `place_on_top` on either one is a reason to change viewpoint and pick a new visible tabletop point. Do not release the candle simply because one surface sample failed; the failed placement keeps it in hand.
For a wreath on a sofa, select a broad, nearly horizontal seat cushion with clear space around the wreath. The coffee table in front of the sofa is a different support, and a sloped sofa arm or backrest can fail stable support even if its pixel belongs to the sofa. Move the viewpoint until the seat is unambiguous before trying another placement.
When staging two bulky jars before filling them, leave two distinct clear support patches. A stool may be too narrow, and a counter already crowded by the first jar may reject the second with `sampling_error`. Inspect the visible free area and choose a broad, empty part of a counter or bar; if the jar is long relative to the surface, try `placement_yaw_degrees` to align its long side with the available length. A failed `place_on_top` keeps the jar held, so move to a materially different support or viewpoint before retrying.

For an instruction to put an item under a fixture, carry the item and select a visible point on that fixture for `place_under`. The executor samples a pose with the simulator's `Under` relation and checks it after settling. A visually nearby floor placement may not satisfy `Under`; inspect the result and continue checking the whole task.

For an instruction to place an item next to a fixture or tree, carry it and select a visible point on that same parent's body for `place_next_to`, not on nearby lawn or floor. The executor searches floor poses around the selected point and accepts one only if the simulator's `NextTo` relation is true after settling. Select the same parent for all items that must be together; inspect each action's result and the final RGB.
If `place_next_to` reports that the parent is unreachable, use `navigate_to` on a visible patch of ground near it, then reselect the parent's body in the new RGB. The ground is an approach target, never the parent for `place_next_to`.
If the tree is seen across furniture or at the image edge, repeated `place_next_to` clicks on nearly the same tree pixel will reuse the same unreachable approach. Move along visible free floor toward a clear side of the tree, inspect the new view, and only then select its trunk again. Keep the gift box held during this recovery.
When close to a Christmas tree, broad branches can fill the camera while the trunk and floor at its base are occluded. A high branch pixel can still make `place_next_to` fail its reach check. Shift to a side view with clear floor beside the tree and select a visible lower trunk or low central branch, then inspect the new RGB before retrying. Do not count a navigation move alone as a successful gift placement.
