# Target selection cases

Consult for thin or curved objects, loaded supports, baskets, shelves above counters, or ambiguous item pixels. The numbered passages below describe local selection and verification, not an obligatory action sequence.

3. Approach the item by marking it in the current RGB. Reinspect after navigation; once its body is clearly visible, call `grasp` on a clearly exposed patch of that body rather than repeatedly navigating toward the same point. For a jar, use the middle of its visible front face. If food must stay on a plate, the plate is the object to grasp: select the white ceramic band outside the colored pizza/topping, on an exposed plate rim away from the tabletop. Check the actual marked pixel against that band before calling `grasp`; a point in the orange pizza center selects the pizza itself even if your intention was the plate. A thin plate at the edge of the image is easy to confuse with the table beneath it: change viewpoint to put the plate near the center and choose an unoccluded rim pixel before grasping. Grasping the food does not guarantee the required `OnTop(food, plate)` relation survives transport. RGB manipulation requires explicit navigation. After an out_of_reach result, approach using navigate_to and reselect the item in fresh RGB; stop approaching once the item is visible and workable. If the body is cropped or hidden, change to a viewpoint that restores visibility. Do not mark the supporting table/floor.
   For a curved or ring-shaped item such as a bratwurst or wreath, put the marked pixel on a thick colored segment of the item itself. Its bounding-box center may be empty space inside the curve or ring; a ray through that space selects the supporting counter or floor even when the item appears centered in the image.
   For an item recessed on a high cabinet shelf, first navigate to visible floor just outside the cabinet opening, then reselect the item's own visible face for `grasp`. A high shelf pixel is not a traversable navigation destination. If an approach reports `navigation_unreachable`, a successful move to the floor in front of that shelf is recovery progress: inspect the new RGB and retry the grasp before concluding the item is unreachable.
   If an upper cabinet is over a counter, the floor directly below its door may be occupied by the counter and cannot be a base destination. Approach from the clear aisle along the counter or around its end, keeping the cabinet visible, then retry `open` or `grasp` on its own current RGB point. A failed floor navigation point near the counter does not prove that the cabinet is unreachable from another side.
   If the upper shelf is cropped after opening the cabinet, step back along the clear aisle and turn to face it until a front or side RGB shows the full shelf and jar bodies before grasping or searching lower cabinets. A close view of only the stovetop, red backsplash, or cabinet lower edge means the high shelf is still outside the image; use another free-floor viewpoint in the aisle and look again. A jar near the top image edge can disappear when you move too close. Keep the food preparation island clear while changing viewpoint: nearby loose ingredients can be disturbed by robot motion.
4. Inspect the new four-camera RGB and action feedback for evidence of the result. On a successful `grasp`, compare `effect.grasped_category` with the item you intended to pick before choosing its destination. That category reports what the selected pixel actually picked, not what you hoped it picked; for example, a `pillar_candle` belongs at the candle destination even if you had mistaken its white body for a candy cane. It does not say which task goals are satisfied. Use the returned RGB to check the intended carried assembly: a plate with one pizza should carry that pizza. If a whole support or unexpected group visibly moves, stage it safely and select the intended item again. Private payload counts are not available in the RGB action feedback. Gripper visibility may be limited; do not infer held-object truth from an unavailable wrist camera. Maintain this as your belief. Carry only one item with the default arm.
   When collecting several loose items into a movable container, keep the container as the destination in your plan. Select an item outside it for each `grasp`, then select the container for `place_inside`. If RGB after a grasp indicates that you picked up the container, release it and reselect a loose item; moving the destination consumes actions and can hide or spill the remaining items.
   A toy box can leave the camera view when you turn toward an item on the same desk. Before concluding it moved, rotate or step back to reframe the desk. In the bedroom with athlete posters, the open toy box is the gray rectangular tub on the desktop; select its interior wall or rim, not the surrounding gray desk, wall, or floor. An `unsupported_relation` result on those surfaces means the box was not selected, so find the tub in fresh RGB before another `place_inside` attempt.
   For items inside an open basket, verify that the exact marked pixel sits on a solid, exposed item patch with visible clearance from wicker and other items on every side. If no current camera shows such a patch, move to a different side of the basket and inspect the new RGB before grasping. In a mixed holiday basket, an exposed white-and-red candy-cane segment may be clearer than the wreath below it; take the clearly visible item first, then reinspect the newly exposed contents. A `bulk_grasp_confirmation_required` error means the selected pixel resolved to a loaded support or furniture-sized object; no grasp occurred. Reinspect the returned RGB and choose the item's solid visible body, not a basket rim, opening, or a pixel through its wicker. Use `confirm_bulk_grasp=true` only if moving the entire container or furniture is your deliberate plan. After a successful grasp, confirm that one item moved independently. If the whole basket moved, stage it on a broad surface or release it over clear floor, then reacquire a visible item patch; the basket has not counted as a completed item.
   For food on a cutting board, select the food's colored body away from the board edge. After grasping, verify the food moved independently; if the board and all its food moved together, stage and release the board before reaching for the food again. If broad-surface staging fails, move over clear visible floor and use `release`, then reacquire each visible food item from its new location. A second grasp while any object remains held is unavailable; a failed board placement alone does not exhaust recovery options.
   If a floor basket looks small and its contents are indistinguishable, use `navigate_to` on the basket body or rim itself, then inspect fresh front and side RGB. A floor point in front of it may leave the basket more than a metre away. If a `grasp` hits the fixed basket or floor, change to a different side and choose an exposed item pixel; do not count that attempt as an item removed.
