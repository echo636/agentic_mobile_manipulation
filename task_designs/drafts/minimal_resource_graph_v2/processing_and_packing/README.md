# Processing and packing materials

Admitted custom development fixture, with complete native feasibility and an
autonomous source-processing success. It is not a released benchmark. Generated
scene, acceptance and runs belong in the workspace experiments directory.

Two intact logs begin on the short counter, a compatible axe on the window
counter, and a carton on the floor beside the window-counter cabinet. Produce
four halves, pack every half in that carton, leave the carton by that cabinet,
return the axe to the short counter, and finish empty-handed. The lid has no
required final state. All legal processing locations, staging, supported
assembly transport and moving the carton remain available.

This is a separate packing task. It does not change the goals or score of the
earlier countertop-delivery experiment. Inside is the natural final relation
for packed products; container capacity and access must be physically verified.

Compare complete methods that process at the source then pack four outputs,
transport two inputs before processing, and reposition the carton for loading.
Count tool staging, access, loading, final carrier placement and verification.
The mechanism is a change in the number of independently handled material
items; no artificial one-log capacity or mandatory carrier route is imposed.

One raw item already admits the processing-location choice. Two raw items add
a shared-tool scheduling decision: prepare both before holding the tool, or
interleave material handling and tool use. Thus this fixture tests two coupled
decisions; it is not a proof that four initial movable objects are globally
minimal. Reducing to one raw item is a mechanism boundary, not an automatic
negative instance for processing-location value.

Nominal direct source processing requires 13 operations: open carton, get axe,
two cuts, return axe, four grasp/place pairs. Processing at the destination
requires 9: open carton, two input grasp/place pairs, get axe, two cuts, return
axe. These are candidate operation ledgers, not verified native costs or an
optimality claim. Navigation, failures, settling and rework remain separate.

The admitted `processing_packing_catalog_input_v1` binds fresh initial-condition
checks and the complete destination-processing reference: 10 calls including a
wait, no failures, all 14 native goals and empty hands. The separate source
reference failed its seventh call during local clearance recovery and remains
failed. Autonomous `rgb_processing_packing_B_r1` subsequently completed that
source-processing method: 16 attempts, 2 failures, 14 successful operations
(13 nominal operations plus closing the carton), all goals and empty hands.
Four output grasp/place pairs include floor retrievals; they are not evidence
of four separate measured cross-area trips.

Public model inputs contain final requirements and general capabilities, not
reference pixels, object IDs, private geometry or the numerical comparison above.
Both arms of the subsequent `rgb_packing_access_*` pair receive the same clarified
cut capability from `../cut_access_v1/`: a visible target in an open container
can be cut subject to ordinary checks. Their planning skill difference is still
only the previously frozen generic method-comparison addition.

That subsequent pair is complete. B uses source processing and finishes with
21 attempts, including 5 failures; C loads the two intact inputs first, cuts
both inside the carton, returns the axe and closes the carton in 10 successful
attempts. Both satisfy all 14 native goals and empty hands. The concrete method
difference is four completed product grasp/place pairs versus two intact-input
pairs: eight handling actions versus four. The full attempt gap also includes
B's interrupted transfer and navigation recovery and C's extra closing action;
it is not entirely a planning-method effect.

The earlier packing C also succeeds, in 33 attempts with 10 failures, after
choosing source processing due to uncertain in-container cutting. It performs
only two explicit successful place_inside calls, with staging, release, box
motion and real multi-object physics; do not infer four separate transfers
from its initial plan. All outcomes remain in the review. This is one complete
development example, not native global optimality or a stable average effect.
